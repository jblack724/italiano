Lecture 1 · Presentazioni e pronuncia

09/02/26 · Week 1 · Introducing yourself, and the pronunciation rules

The whole lesson is four questions and the verbs that answer them, plus the rules for how c, g, and sc are pronounced. Everything else on the slides was practice or admin.

Le quattro domande (the four questions)

Learn these as pairs. The question you are asked decides the form you answer with.

Ask someone (tu)You answer (io)Ask about someone (lui/lei)You answer
Come ti chiami?
What's your name?		Mi chiamo…Come si chiama?Si chiama…
Di dove sei?
Where are you from?		Sono di…Di dov'è?È di…
Che cosa studi?
What do you study?		Studio…Che cosa studia?Studia…
Come stai?
How are you?		Sto…Come sta?Sta…

Say Piacere! ("nice to meet you") when you meet someone, and drop the subject pronoun. Sono di Boston, not Io sono di Boston. The verb ending already says who you mean.

Two spellings to watch. Di dov'è? is Di dove è? with the e of dove dropped. And è with the accent means "is"; e without it means "and".

Put together, an introduction runs: Piacere! Mi chiamo Justin. Sono di ______. Studio ______. Oggi sto ______.

Three things make these easier to hold on to:

  • Studiare is regular. For io, tu, lui/lei, every regular -are verb ends -o, -i, -a.
  • Essere and stare are irregular. There is no pattern; memorize them.
  • Chiamarsi is reflexive, so two things change: the pronoun in front (mi, ti, si, ci, vi, si) and the ending.

The slides drilled these with pictures: Si chiama Dante. Sta male. È di Firenze. and Si chiama Anna. Sta benissimo. È di Roma.

Come stai? Le risposte (how you're doing)

Memorize the scale in order, best to worst.

  1. benissimo / molto bene great / very well
  2. bene well
  3. così così so-so
  4. non bene not well
  5. male badly

Add oggi ("today") to say how you are right now: Oggi sto benissimo!

Pick the one or two you need for yourself; recognize the rest. Nearly all are near-cognates.

Every one of these is feminine. Singular ones take la (or l' before a vowel); le scienze is plural. The two worth extra attention are l'ingegneria (een-jeh-nyeh-REE-ah) and la fisica, where the stress falls on the first syllable, FEE-zee-kah.

Pronuncia: la regola dura/morbida (the hard/soft rule)

This is the rule the whole pronunciation half of the lecture was built on. It governs c, g, and sc, and it never changes.

Before a, o, u the sound is hard. Before e, i it is soft.

To keep it hard in front of e or i, Italian inserts an h: che, chi, ghe, ghi, sche, schi. To make it soft in front of a, o, u, it inserts an i: cia, cio, ciu, gia, gio, giu, scia, scio, sciu. That inserted letter is silent; it only sets the sound.

In classe (practice, nothing to memorize)

  • Interview a classmate with the four questions and write the answers down, then introduce them to the class: Lui/Lei si chiama… è di… studia… sta…. Every verb moves to the lui/lei form.
  • Brainstorm Italian words you already know. The slide's examples were all food and culture cognates: cappuccino, casinò, spaghetti, paparazzi, pizza, gelato, broccoli, soprano, pianoforte, panino, bruschetta.
  • Tongue twisters (scioglilingua), for the sounds above. Say them out loud; do not memorize them.
    • Trentatré trentini entrarono in Trento, tutti e trentatré trotterellando. rolled r
    • Sopra la panca la capra campa, sotto la panca la capra crepa. hard c
    • Se sei gnomi mangiano sei gnocchi con gli occhi, con gli occhi quanti gnocchi mangia ogni gnomo? gn, gli, cchi
    • Apelle figlio di Apollo fece una palla di pelle di pollo. gli, double l

Il corso (course rules worth remembering)

  • No translation tools of any kind. No Google Translate, ChatGPT, or other LLMs, no native speakers, no co-writing. Allowed: the textbook, wordreference.com, italian-verbs.com. If you use grammar the class has not covered, be ready to explain what it does and where you found it.
  • Flipped classroom. Study the unit before class, because class time is for speaking. The course moves fast and leans on memorization.
  • In class, speak Italian. English is for logistics only. No screens.
  • Ask early. Say something before you miss a deadline, and there is no penalty for asking about a policy.
  • Instructor: Vincenzo Dimaggio, vdimaggio@fas.harvard.edu. Student hours Boylston 332, Monday and Thursday 3:00 to 4:00, or by appointment on Zoom. Materials are on Canvas and on VHL.
