Lecture 1 · Presentazioni e pronuncia

09/02/26 · Week 1 · Introducing yourself, and the pronunciation rules

Four questions and the verbs that answer them, plus the rules for how c, g, and sc are pronounced.

Le quattro domande (the four questions)

Learn these as pairs. The question you are asked decides the form you answer with.

Ask someone (tu)You answer (io)Ask about someone (lui/lei)You answer
Come ti chiami?
What's your name?		Mi chiamo…Come si chiama?Si chiama…
Di dove sei?
Where are you from?		Sono di…Di dov'è?È di…
Che cosa studi?
What do you study?		Studio…Che cosa studia?Studia…
Come stai?
How are you?		Sto…Come sta?Sta…

Say Piacere! ("nice to meet you") when you meet someone, and drop the subject pronoun. Sono di Boston, not Io sono di Boston. The verb ending already says who you mean.

Two spellings to watch. Di dov'è? is Di dove è? with the e of dove dropped. And è with the accent means "is"; e without it means "and".

Put together, an introduction runs: Piacere! Mi chiamo Justin. Sono di ______. Studio ______. Oggi sto ______.

Three things make these easier to hold on to:

  • Studiare is regular. For io, tu, lui/lei, every regular -are verb ends -o, -i, -a.
  • Essere and stare are irregular. There is no pattern; memorize them.
  • Chiamarsi is reflexive, so two things change: the pronoun in front (mi, ti, si, ci, vi, si) and the ending.
  • Si chiama Dante. Sta male. È di Firenze. His name is Dante. He's not well. He's from Florence.
  • Si chiama Anna. Sta benissimo. È di Roma. Her name is Anna. She's doing great. She's from Rome.

Come stai? Le risposte (how you're doing)

Memorize the scale in order, best to worst.

  1. benissimo / molto bene great / very well
  2. bene well
  3. così così so-so
  4. non bene not well
  5. male badly

Add oggi ("today") to say how you are right now: Oggi sto benissimo!

Every one of these is feminine. Singular ones take la (or l' before a vowel); le scienze is plural. The two worth extra attention are l'ingegneria (een-jeh-nyeh-REE-ah) and la fisica, where the stress falls on the first syllable, FEE-zee-kah.

Pronuncia: la regola dura/morbida (the hard/soft rule)

This is the rule the whole pronunciation half of the lecture was built on. It governs c, g, and sc, and it never changes.

Before a, o, u the sound is hard. Before e, i it is soft.

To keep it hard in front of e or i, Italian inserts an h: che, chi, ghe, ghi, sche, schi. To make it soft in front of a, o, u, it inserts an i: cia, cio, ciu, gia, gio, giu, scia, scio, sciu. That inserted letter is silent; it only sets the sound.
